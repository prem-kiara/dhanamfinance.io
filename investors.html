Investment Process

How to Invest in Dhanam

Investing in Dhanam NCDs is simple and can be done through our branches or online. Follow these steps to get started.

1

Contact Our Investor Relations Team

Reach out to us via phone at 1800 2025 180, WhatsApp, email, or visit your nearest Dhanam branch. Our team will walk you through all available NCD schemes and help you choose the best fit.

2

Select Your Scheme & Tenure

Choose from our four NCD schemes — Doubling, Annual, Cumulative, or Monthly — based on your financial goals. Pick a tenure from 13 to 60 months. Our team will provide a detailed illustration of your expected returns.

3

Submit KYC & Demat Documents

Provide your identity proof, address proof, PAN card, bank details, and Demat account details (DP ID & Client ID). NCDs are allotted in demat form, so an active Demat account is mandatory.

4

Transfer Funds & Sign Agreement

Transfer the investment amount via NEFT/RTGS or cheque. Review and sign the NCD subscription agreement. All terms, interest rates, and maturity details are clearly outlined before you sign.

5

NCDs Credited to Your Demat Account

Once your application is processed and funds received, your NCDs will be allotted and credited directly to your Demat account. You'll receive a confirmation with your NCD certificate details, ISIN number, and maturity schedule.

6

Receive Interest & Maturity Payout

Interest payouts (monthly/annual/cumulative) and principal redemption at maturity are credited directly to your registered bank account via NEFT. You'll be notified ahead of every payout.

Why Demat Form?

  • Secure & Paperless — No risk of physical certificates being lost, stolen, or damaged
  • Easy Tracking — View your NCD holdings anytime through your Demat account statement
  • Transferable — NCDs held in demat can be transferred to another demat account if needed
  • Regulatory Compliant — Demat allotment is mandated by RBI and SEBI for investor protection

Don't have a Demat account?

You can open a Demat account with any SEBI-registered depository participant (DP) such as Zerodha, Groww, Angel One, ICICI Direct, HDFC Securities, etc. The process is fully online and typically completed in 1–2 days.

Our team can guide you through the process.

Risks & Safety

We believe in full transparency about the risks involved:

  • Credit risk — borrowers defaulting on loans we've given them.
  • Liquidity risk — NCDs are typically held to maturity; early exit may not be available.
  • Business-cycle risk — the NBFC sector, like any sector, has cycles.
  • Leverage risk — since we borrow to lend, our financial health matters.

Please read the full risk disclosure in the debenture offer document before investing.

Dhanam is currently a privately held company, meaning our equity shares are not traded on a stock exchange. Our NCDs, however, are held in dematerialised (demat) form, which provides transparency and secure digital ownership records — regardless of the company's listing status.

Bank FDs are covered by DICGC deposit insurance up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank, and are backed by scheduled banks under stricter RBI regulation — making them among the safest investment instruments available. Dhanam NCDs offer higher returns in exchange for a different risk profile — they are not DICGC-covered and their performance is tied to Dhanam's business. Many investors use NCDs as a higher-yield complement to FDs rather than a replacement, maintaining a diversified portfolio.

In an adverse scenario, interest payments could be delayed and, in a severe case, capital could be at risk. To mitigate this, our NCDs are secured — backed by a charge on company receivables and assets — which provides an additional layer of protection compared to unsecured instruments. We also maintain conservative lending practices and prudent asset-liability management to minimise such risks.

No investment is completely risk-free. Bank FDs come closest due to deposit insurance, but even they have limitations. We believe an informed investor is the best investor — so we're upfront that our NCDs carry risk in exchange for higher returns. Our role is to give you complete transparency so you can decide if the risk-reward suits your goals.

Who Should Invest & How Much

Our NCDs are best suited for investors who:

  • Have a moderate-to-high risk appetite.
  • Are looking for higher returns than traditional FDs.
  • Understand how NBFCs operate and what drives their returns.
  • Can commit capital for the full chosen tenure without needing early access.

Dhanam NCDs are not suitable for investors who:

  • May need to withdraw funds at any time before maturity.
  • Are planning to invest their entire life savings in a single instrument.
  • Prefer DICGC-insured instruments exclusively.
  • Are uncomfortable with the risk profile of NBFC-sector debt.

In such cases, bank FDs, government savings schemes, or post-office instruments may be more appropriate.

We recommend investing only a portion of your overall portfolio. Diversification across asset classes — equity, debt, gold, real estate, bank deposits — is the foundation of prudent wealth building. Our minimum investment per NCD is ₹1 lakh, but investing well within your overall plan matters more than any single ticket size.

Yes — absolutely. Never concentrate all your funds into one company, one instrument, or one sector. Even within NCDs, spreading across issuers reduces concentration risk. A well-diversified portfolio is the single biggest risk-mitigation tool any investor has.

Exit, Maturity & Alternatives

NCDs are fixed-tenure instruments and are generally held to maturity. Premature exit, where permitted by the scheme, is subject to the terms of the debenture and may involve an interest adjustment. Please plan your investment in line with your liquidity needs and speak with our team about any specific scheme's terms.

Since our NCDs are held in demat form, limited transferability may be possible, depending on the specific scheme's terms. Please refer to the debenture's transfer conditions or speak with our investor-relations team for the specifics of your holding.

At maturity, your principal is repaid in full along with the final interest payment, directly credited to your registered bank account. The amount, timing, and mode of repayment are pre-defined in the offer document — no paperwork or chase required at your end.

Among the safest debt-type alternatives are bank Fixed Deposits (DICGC-insured up to ₹5 lakh), Post Office Schemes, and Government Savings Schemes. NCDs like ours offer higher returns in exchange for a different risk profile. A balanced portfolio often contains a mix of both — the key is matching each instrument to a specific role in your financial plan.
